\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Everyday Higher Order Thinking}
Benjamin wants to create an action opportunity for his students. To do so, he needs to generate a table of values for the function $p(x) = -x^3 + 11x^2 - 7x - 69$, as shown here:

\small

\directlua{

    tex.print("\\begin{wraptable}{l}{8em}\\vspace{-2em}\\begin{tabular}{|c|c|}\\hline x&y \\\\\\hline");

    for i=3545,3555 do

        x = i/1000;

        y = -x^3+11*x^2-7*x-69;

        y = math.floor(y*1000+0.5)/1000;

        tex.print(x.."&"..y.."\\\\\\hline");

    end;

    tex.print("\\end{tabular}\\end{wraptable}")

}

\Large

Imagine that Benjamin will need to revise the problem frequently---for example, by changing the function or the number of rows in the table.

If you were Benjamin, how would you go about generating this table? \textbf{\color{red} Briefly describe your strategy and explain the factors you would consider when choosing it, such as ease of revision, accuracy, and efficiency.}

\end{frame}
\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Everyday Higher Order Thinking}
Create two polynomials such that 
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=(\alph*)]
\item
They both have $x$-intercepts only at $-2$, $1$ and $4$, but not anywhere else.
\item 
They have very different general shapes.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{\stretch{1}}
Use a graphing utility to check your candidates. \textbf{\color{red}Be sure to write down both the formulas and sketch the graph on a coordinate plane.}

Explain your thought process to your group members meticulously.
\end{frame}
